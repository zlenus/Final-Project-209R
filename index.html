How wildfires affect insurance premiums and their renewal rates

Since 2017, California’s average homeowner premium has climbed by about two thirds, and insurers have dropped more policies than they’ve written. This map shows where that pressure lands, ZIP code by ZIP code, next to the fires that came before it.

Premiums bent upward after 2017

For a decade the statewide average premium barely moved, rising about $130 between 2008 and 2017. Then came the Tubbs and Thomas fires in 2017 and the Camp Fire in 2018. Over the next six years the average climbed from roughly $1,075 to $1,787.

Closer to a burn scar, more likely to be dropped

Between 2015 and 2022, policies in ZIP codes whose center sat inside a burn area were non-renewed at 11.4%, compared with under 9% for ZIPs more than 30 km from a fire. The gap is small statewide, but it concentrates in a handful of places.

In 2023, drops outpaced new policies

New policies and non-renewals tracked each other through 2022. In 2023 new policies fell to about 724,000 while non-renewals stayed near 788,000, the first year in this span that more coverage left the market than entered it.

Twelve ZIPs where non-renewals tripled

In Paradise-area ZIP 95954, nearly half of policies were not renewed in 2023. Most of these places are foothill and mountain communities in Butte, Shasta, Tulare and Kern counties, plus two in eastern San Diego County.

What starts the fires

Lightning accounts for the most acreage burned since 2000, about 9 million acres, much of it from the 2020 lightning siege. Power lines rank fourth by acreage but sit behind several of the most destructive fires near homes, which matters more to insurers than acreage alone.
